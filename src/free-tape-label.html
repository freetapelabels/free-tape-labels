    User guide

    How to create your LTO barcode labels in a few minutes. Click a chapter to expand it.

    1. Define your labels
    1. At the top, choose the cartridge: LTO or IBM 3592.
    2. Choose the media type, e.g. L9 for LTO-9. It becomes the last two characters of the barcode.
    3. Enter the first label (6 characters, e.g. VOL100) and the count. Right below you see which labels will be created.
    4. The digits at the end count up automatically. Under “Which characters count up?” you can allow more positions, e.g. so the letter in front continues after 999.
    5. If you already have a list, choose “Own list” and paste the IDs, one per line. A CSV export from your backup software works too.
    2. Choose labels and printer
    1. Choose “Label sheet” for laser or inkjet printers, or “Label printer” for rolls, e.g. Zebra or Brother QL.
    2. Click the template that matches your labels. Below the selection is a note about the template, e.g. whether the labels need trimming.
    3. Your labels are not listed? Under “Custom template or adjust dimensions” enter paper size, margins and label size in millimetres.
    4. Dimensions help with a custom template: click into a dimension field and the preview shows exactly that measurement. Turn on “Show dimensions in the preview” to see all of them at once.
    3. Design the look
    • Layout: choose where text and barcode go. The preview shows the result immediately.
    • Description: e.g. “Infrastructure Backup” with an icon. Placeholders such as {date} or {no} are filled in when printing.
    • Under “Preset” you will find ready-made descriptions, e.g. for offsite, archive, WORM or cleaning cartridges. Good to know: {date} is the date the label was printed, not the date of the backup.
    • Own logo: under “Icon”, choose “Own logo” and upload a PNG, JPG or SVG file. The logo is scaled down, stays in your browser and appears in all layouts except “LTO classic”. On label printers and in the “Black & white” style it is printed in greyscale.
    • Colour: colours fill the character boxes and help you spot cartridges quickly. For label printers colours are switched off because they only print black.
    • Border: “No border” for pre-cut labels, “Crop marks” or “Dashed” if you cut them yourself.
    • You normally do not need to change the advanced barcode settings. The defaults follow the specification.
    4. Print
    1. Check the status above the preview. “Ready to print” means everything is fine. If there are warnings, the list below the preview explains what to do.
    2. Click “Create PDF” and open the PDF.
    3. In Chrome and Edge, the app first asks where to save the PDF. Other browsers put it in your downloads folder. The file name contains the template, the first label and the date.
    4. Print at “Actual size” / 100 %, never “Fit to page”. Otherwise the dimensions are wrong and the library may not read the barcodes.
    5. The first time, make a test print on plain paper and hold it against a label sheet.
    6. Partly used sheet? Enter the first free label under “Start at label no.”. Used positions are skipped.
    5. Calibrate your printer

    Many printers print one or two millimetres off. You correct this once, and every print fits from then on.

    1. Under “Print position”, open “Calibrate printer” and click “Create calibration sheet”.
    2. Print the sheet at 100 % on plain paper.

    Method 1: with a ruler

    1. Measure the distance from the left and top paper edges to the lines. The target is 20.0 mm each.
    2. New offset = previous offset + (20.0 − measured value). Example: previously 0, measured 21.5 mm gives −1.5 mm.

    Method 2: without a ruler (folding)

    1. Fold the sheet so that the left edge lies exactly on the right edge. Where the fold crosses scale A, read off the left correction.
    2. Then fold it so that the top edge lies exactly on the bottom edge. Where the fold crosses scale B, read off the top correction.
    3. Enter the values exactly as you read them. They replace the previous correction.

    One of the two methods is enough. To check, print the sheet again: both lines are then at 20.0 mm and the creases at 0.

    6. Avoid duplicate barcodes

    Each ID may exist only once in a tape library. The app helps in two ways:

    • After each PDF the app asks whether you will use the labels. If you choose “Yes, remember”, it warns you if you create one of these IDs again later.
    • In step 1, under “Avoid duplicate barcodes”, you can load the cartridge list from your backup software, e.g. a CSV from Veeam or Backup Exec. The app finds the barcode column itself and warns about overlaps.
    • You can export the remembered labels as CSV and import them again, e.g. for colleagues.
    7. What is saved

    The app saves everything automatically in your browser as soon as you change something. On your next visit everything is set up as before:

    • Calibration (left and top offset)
    • Colour style including custom colours, border, line width and layout
    • Description and icon
    • Own logo
    • Template including custom template, cartridge and media type
    • First label, count and start position
    • Barcode settings and language
    • Remembered labels and loaded inventory

    Good to know: the data is stored only in this browser on this computer and is not sent anywhere. In another browser, on another computer or in a private window the app starts empty. If you clear your browser data, the settings are gone. To back up or move to another computer, open the “Project” menu at the top and choose “Save project”. This saves a file (e.g. FreeTapeLabels_Project_herma4459_VOL001_2026-10-14.json) containing all settings including calibration, colours and your custom template. Store it e.g. on a network share or pass it on to colleagues. Use “Open project” to load it again, also on another computer or in another browser. Remembered labels are not included in this file; back them up under “Avoid duplicate barcodes” with “Export (CSV)”. To start over, the “Project” menu has two entries: “Reset settings” returns all settings to the initial state, “Delete everything” also removes remembered labels and the inventory. There is no step-by-step undo; if you want to experiment, save a backup with “Save project” first.

    Tips: labeling backup tapes the right way

    A well-thought-out scheme saves time in the data center and prevents mix-ups. These basic rules have proven themselves:

    • Consistent naming scheme. The six VOLSER characters usually consist of a short prefix and a sequential number, e.g. BKP001, or of a code per site. Use the same scheme in all libraries.
    • Every ID only once. Duplicate barcodes confuse the library and the backup software. Check new labels against your inventory before printing.
    • Purpose in the description, not in the ID. Pools such as “Weekly” or “Offsite” change, a cartridge’s ID stays the same. So put the purpose in the description next to the barcode.
    • No backup date on the label. Cartridges get reused. The backup software manages content and retention; the label shows at most the print date.
    • Mark WORM and cleaning cartridges. They have their own media types, e.g. LZ for LTO-9 WORM or CU for cleaning. A separate description or color keeps them out of the wrong pool.
    • Durable material. For libraries and offsite tapes, polyester labels are more robust than paper.
    • Apply carefully. Stick the label straight into the recess provided, without overhang and never over an old label.
    • Test first, then print. Print one sheet on plain paper, let the library read one label and only then print the rest.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I print LTO labels myself?

    Choose the cartridge and media type, enter the first label and the count, pick your label sheet and click “Create PDF”. Print the PDF at “Actual size” / 100 %. Before printing on labels for the first time, make a test print on plain paper and hold it against a label sheet.

    What does L8 or L9 at the end of the barcode mean?

    The last two characters are the media type: L8 stands for LTO-8, L9 for LTO-9, LA for LTO-10. WORM cartridges have their own codes such as LZ, cleaning cartridges CU or C1 to C5. The first six characters are the actual cartridge ID, the VOLSER.

    How should I name LTO tapes?

    The VOLSER has six characters; the uppercase letters A to Z and the digits 0 to 9 are allowed. A short prefix plus a sequential number has proven itself, e.g. BKP001 to BKP999. The purpose of a cartridge, such as offsite or monthly, is better placed in the description because it can change.

    Where does the label go on an LTO cartridge?

    Into the recess provided on the narrow back of the cartridge, the side that faces outward in the library. Apply it straight and without overhang so the barcode reader picks it up reliably and the cartridge doesn’t jam in the drive.

    Which printer works best?

    A 600 dpi laser printer produces the most reliable barcodes on label sheets. For single labels, thermal transfer printers such as Zebra work well; a Brother QL with DK-11203 works with trimming. Inkjet printers also work, but ink can spread on some labels.

    My library does not read the label. What now?

    Most common causes: the PDF was printed scaled down (make sure to print at 100 %), the label is crooked or overlaps the label area, or the toner is too light. Also check that the media type matches the cartridge. If that does not help, try the bar width reduction under “Advanced barcode settings”.

    Which labels do I need?

    For A4, Avery Zweckform 3420 and Herma 4459 are common; for Letter, Avery 6571/6577 and OnlineLabels OL173. For long life in a library, polyester labels such as Avery L4775 or OL173LP are better than paper.

    What is the best LTO label software?

    That depends on what you need. What matters are barcodes that follow the IBM specification, templates for your labels, a check before printing, and the price. Free Tape Labels covers all of this for free and without installation.

    Are the colors easy to tell apart for colorblind users?

    Yes. The default colors are based on the Okabe-Ito palette, which was designed for people with color vision deficiency such as red-green color blindness. In addition, every character is always printed as text on the label; color is only a help when searching the shelf. You can set your own colors under “Custom style”, and for black-and-white printers there is the “Black & white” style.
